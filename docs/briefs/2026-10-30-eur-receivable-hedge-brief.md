# Executive Brief: Foreign Exchange Hedging

## Currency Exposure and Business Risk
Our company expects to receive €4,500,000 in one year. Because the payment is in euros but our financial reporting is in U.S. dollars, the final dollar amount will depend on the EUR/USD exchange rate at settlement. This creates uncertainty in our future cash flow and could affect our budget and financial planning.
For example, at an exchange rate of 1.00, the payment is worth $4,500,000. If the rate falls to 0.95, the payment is worth only $4,275,000, a decrease of $225,000. This demonstrates how an unfavorable currency movement can reduce the value of a foreign-currency receivable even when the euro amount remains unchanged.
## Hedging Alternatives
Three strategies will be evaluated to determine how effectively they manage this exposure.
Forward contract: A forward contract locks in an exchange rate today for converting euros into dollars at a future date. Its main advantage is certainty because the company can estimate its dollar proceeds in advance. However, the company gives up the opportunity to benefit from a more favorable exchange rate if the euro strengthens. Counterparty risk and contractual terms must also be considered.
Money-market hedge: This strategy uses borrowing, currency conversion, and investing to establish a more predictable dollar outcome. It can provide certainty similar to a forward contract. However, it involves more steps, depends on borrowing and investment rates, and uses borrowing capacity that could be needed elsewhere in the business.
Put option: A put option gives the company the right, but not the obligation, to sell euros at a predetermined exchange rate. It protects against unfavorable currency movements while allowing the company to benefit if the euro strengthens. Its main disadvantage is the upfront premium, which reduces net proceeds whether or not the option is exercised.
## Assumptions and Hypothesis
The initial analysis assumes the company will receive the full €4,500,000 in one year. The payment amount and timing are assumed to remain unchanged, and exchange rates are expressed in U.S. dollars per euro. The analysis will incorporate relevant interest rates, forward rates, option premiums, and transaction costs when reliable market data becomes available.
I expect the forward contract to provide the clearest budget certainty. The money-market hedge may produce a comparable outcome depending on interest rates and costs, while the put option may offer greater upside if the euro strengthens enough to offset its premium. This hypothesis would be challenged if the model shows that another strategy produces materially better net dollar proceeds after accounting for costs and risk.
