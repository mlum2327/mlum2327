1. Problem Statement

The company expects to receive €4,500,000 in one year. Because the receivable is denominated in euros while the company's functional and reporting currency is U.S. dollars, the final USD value will depend on the EUR exchange rate at settlement. An unfavorable decline in the euro could reduce the company's dollar proceeds and create uncertainty in budgeting and financial planning. The objective of this model is to compare forward contracts, money-market hedges, and EUR put options to evaluate how effectively each strategy protects the receivable while balancing certainty, potential upside, and hedging costs.

The model will support a corporate treasury decision by evaluating the expected USD outcomes under different future exchange-rate scenarios.

3. Assumptions & Constraints

Exposure: The company will receive €4,500,000 in one year.

Functional currency: U.S. dollars.

Quote convention: All exchange rates are expressed as USD per EUR. A higher quote means the euro has appreciated against the dollar.

Horizon: Single-maturity model with a 365-day settlement horizon.

Day-count basis: The model will use the documented day-count conventions associated with the sourced USD and EUR interest rates. Separate USD and EUR bases may be used if required.

Forward hedge: The company sells euros forward to lock in a USD conversion rate.

Money-market hedge: The company borrows euros today, converts the borrowed euros into USD at the spot rate, and invests the USD until settlement. The euro receivable repays the foreign-currency borrowing.

Option hedge: The company purchases a EUR put option to establish a minimum conversion rate while retaining the ability to benefit from euro appreciation.

Option premium: Paid upfront in USD and carried forward to settlement using the USD interest rate.

Parity: The money-market hedge is assumed to approximate the forward hedge under covered interest-rate parity.

Transaction costs: Excluded from the initial base case until reliable market data is available. They will be considered as a sensitivity factor.

Counterparty risk: Excluded from the initial model.

Tax and accounting: Excluded. The model reports pre-tax cash outcomes.

Scenario construction: Future spot rates are varied deterministically without probability weights.

4. Calculation Flow

The model evaluates a EUR receivable. The primary objective is to compare the USD proceeds generated by each hedging strategy.

Step 1 — Derived Inputs

DF_USD = 1 + R_USD × T_DAYS / BASIS

DF_FC = 1 + R_FC × T_DAYS / BASIS

FV_PREM_PUT = −PREM_PUT × FC_AMT × DF_USD

Step 2 — Forward Hedge

USD_FWD = FC_AMT × F0_in

The forward contract locks in the USD proceeds at inception and remains constant across all future spot-rate scenarios.

Step 3 — Money-Market Hedge

Borrow FC_AMT / DF_FC euros today.

Convert the borrowed euros into USD at the initial spot rate.

Invest the USD proceeds until settlement.

Calculate:

USD_MM = (FC_AMT / DF_FC) × S0_in × DF_USD

At maturity, the EUR receivable repays the foreign-currency borrowing.

Parity check: USD_MM should approximately equal USD_FWD under covered interest-rate parity.

Step 4 — Put Option Hedge

The company purchases a EUR put option with strike K_PUT.

At settlement:

USD_PUT(S_T) = MAX(S_T, K_PUT) × FC_AMT + FV_PREM_PUT

This strategy protects the minimum USD conversion value while preserving the opportunity to benefit from euro appreciation. The premium reduces the final net proceeds.

6. Model Review — What Worked & What to Improve

6.1 What Worked

Stage 1 clearly identifies the company's EUR receivable exposure and the potential effect of unfavorable exchange-rate movements.

Three hedging strategies provide a basis for comparing certainty, flexibility, and hedging costs.

The model uses a consistent USD-per-EUR quotation convention.

The sensitivity analysis will allow the company to evaluate different future exchange-rate outcomes.

6.2 What to Improve

Standardize all Excel named ranges to improve formula consistency and auditability.

Incorporate sourced and timestamped spot rates, forward rates, interest rates, and option premiums.

Verify covered interest-rate parity between the forward and money-market hedges.

Include a sensitivity grid controlled by STEP_FRAC and GRID_BAND.

Add a sensitivity chart comparing all four strategies.

Evaluate transaction costs when reliable market data becomes available.

Independently verify formulas and base-case results before preparing the final recommendation.

6.3 Auditability Checklist

Every input has a standardized named range.

Every formula uses named ranges rather than hard-coded cell references.

Money-market hedge ties to the forward hedge within 0.05%, subject to market input consistency.

Put payoff at S_T = K_PUT equals K_PUT × FC_AMT + FV_PREM_PUT.

Sensitivity grid is symmetric around S_T = S0_in.

Grid is driven by STEP_FRAC and GRID_BAND.

Notes tab records market data sources and access dates.

Cell colors match the legend: yellow inputs, blue assumptions, black formulas, green cross-tab links.

7. Sensitivity Plan

Grid: S_T_grid spans S0_in × (1 ± 5%) in 1% increments, producing 11 scenarios.

Strategies plotted: No hedge, forward, money market, and EUR put option.

Primary chart: Line chart with future EUR/USD conversion rate on the x-axis and USD proceeds on the y-axis.

Secondary table: Hedge profit compared with the unhedged position.

What the chart should communicate: The trade-off between certainty from forward and money-market hedges, optionality from the put option, and the exposure to currency movements from remaining unhedged.

8. Limitations & Next Steps

Limitations.

The initial model does not incorporate:

Partial, layered, or dynamic hedging.

Credit and counterparty risk.

Implied-volatility-based option pricing.

Hedge accounting treatment under ASC 815 or IFRS 9.

Multi-currency or multi-horizon portfolio effects.

Next steps:

Stage 3: Build the Excel workbook using the named ranges and calculation flow in this specification.

Stage 4: Populate the workbook with sourced and timestamped market data.

Stage 5: Independently validate the results and prepare a decision memo recommending the most appropriate hedging strategy.

The original Stage 1 hypothesis will be evaluated against the actual model results rather than assumed to be correct.

9. Writing a Strong Specification

The specification should function as a professional handoff document. It should use consistent terminology, clearly defined variables, reproducible formulas, and documented assumptions. Another treasury analyst or AI assistant should be able to rebuild the model using this document.

The specification will also support the final CFO decision memo by providing a clear explanation of the exposure, calculation methodology, and analytical outputs.
